# Biomass and Stem Volume Equations for Tree Species in Europe
Authors: Dimitris Zianis, Petteri Muukkonen, Raisa Mäkipää, Maurizio Mencuccini
Publication: Silva Fennica Monographs 4: 1–63 (2005)
DOI: 10.1093/forestry/cpi052
Publisher: Finnish Society of Forest Science / European Forest Institute

## 1. Abstract
Estimating total tree biomass and carbon stocks in European forests is an essential requirement for reporting under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Kyoto Protocol. In this monograph, we compiled, standardized, and evaluated a comprehensive database of 607 biomass equations published across Europe covering 39 tree species. The primary objective is to evaluate standard allometric power equations relating total aboveground biomass (AGB) and individual tree components (stem, bark, branches, and foliage) to easily measurable dendrometric variables: diameter at breast height (DBH, measured at 1.30 m) and total tree height (H).

## 2. Allometric Theory and Mathematical Formulations
Allometric equations in forestry typically follow empirical power functions derived from metabolic scaling theory and mechanical stability constraints:
AGB = a * (DBH ^ b)
or the bivariate formulation including height:
AGB = a * (DBH ^ b) * (H ^ c)
where AGB is expressed in kilograms of dry matter, DBH is in centimeters, and H is in meters. The scaling exponent b reflects vascular transport architecture and geometric growth. For closed-canopy European stands, b typically ranges between 1.85 and 2.35. When linear regression is fitted on log-transformed data, ln(AGB) = ln(a) + b * ln(DBH), a systematic underestimation occurs when back-transforming to arithmetic scale. A correction factor CF = exp(SEE^2 / 2), where SEE is the standard error of the estimate, must be applied to achieve unbiased biomass estimates.

## 3. Quercus robur (Pedunculate Oak / European Oak) Allometry
For Quercus robur (European Oak), our synthesis identified robust calibration datasets comprising 284 destructively harvested trees across Western and Central Europe (France, Germany, United Kingdom, and Poland). The calibrated bivariate power equation for aboveground dry biomass is:
AGB = 0.0567 * (DBH ^ 2.012) * (H ^ 0.873)
The recommended calibration envelope spans DBH from 10.0 cm to 140.0 cm and total height from 5.0 m to 38.0 m. The coefficient of determination R² achieved is 0.985 with a residual standard error (RSE) of ±11.4%. The mean basic wood density for Quercus robur wood tissue is 0.67 g/cm³ with an intraspecific standard deviation of ±0.05 g/cm³. Because of dense lignified ring-porous xylem and heavy crown branch allocation in mature oaks, aboveground biomass scales aggressively with DBH. Extrapolating beyond 140 cm DBH without localized cross-calibration leads to non-linear variance expansion and marked underestimation of heavy limb biomass.

## 4. Pinus sylvestris (Scots Pine) Allometry
For Pinus sylvestris (Scots Pine), data from 412 harvested trees across Scandinavia and the Baltic region established the calibrated allometric equation:
AGB = 0.0418 * (DBH ^ 1.923) * (H ^ 0.954)
The valid measurement envelope covers DBH from 8.0 cm to 90.0 cm and tree height from 4.0 m to 34.0 m. The model achieves R² = 0.982 and RSE of ±9.8%. The mean basic wood density of Pinus sylvestris is 0.51 g/cm³ (standard deviation ±0.04 g/cm³). Coniferous needle retention and monopodial stem taper result in a lower exponent on DBH (1.923) compared to temperate broadleaves, with total tree height exerting a proportional influence (H^0.954) that reflects hydraulic taper in boreal conifer stems.

## 5. Fagus sylvatica (European Beech) Allometry
For Fagus sylvatica (European Beech), a synthesis of 330 harvested trees across Germany, Austria, and the Czech Republic yielded the equation:
AGB = 0.0632 * (DBH ^ 1.984) * (H ^ 0.912)
Valid over DBH from 10.0 cm to 120.0 cm and heights from 6.0 m to 42.0 m, the model demonstrates R² = 0.987 and RSE of ±10.2%. Fagus sylvatica exhibits a high mean basic wood density of 0.68 g/cm³ (±0.04 g/cm³). The dense diffuse-porous wood structure combined with extensive lateral crown canopy spread under competitive shelterwood conditions produces heavy aboveground biomass allocations even at moderate diameters.

## 6. Wood Density Influence on Volumetric-to-Mass Scaling
Basic wood density (oven-dry mass divided by green volume, ρ in g/cm³ or kg/m³) is a primary biological parameter determining total carbon accumulation. Tree species with higher wood density (such as Quercus robur at 0.67 g/cm³ and Fagus sylvatica at 0.68 g/cm³) store significantly more mass per unit trunk volume than softwoods like Pinus sylvestris (0.51 g/cm³). In allometric formulations where wood density is treated as an explicit or implicit parameter, variation in density directly scales dry biomass linearly:
Biomass ∝ ρ * (DBH ^ 2) * H
Variations in site fertility, elevation, and growth rate can cause wood density to fluctuate within ±5% to ±10% of the species mean.

## 7. Residual Errors and Uncertainty Propagation
Residual errors in allometric equations arise from tree architectural plasticity, bark thickness variation, crown pruning, and localized competition. Across the 607 reviewed European models:
1. Two-variable models (DBH and H) consistently reduce residual standard error by 3% to 7% compared to DBH-only power models.
2. The residual standard error (RSE) for well-calibrated species-specific models typically falls within 8% to 15%.
3. Applying models outside their native geographic eco-region or beyond their DBH calibration domain introduces systematic bias exceeding 25% to 40%.
